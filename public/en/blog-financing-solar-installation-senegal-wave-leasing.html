How to finance a solar installation in Senegal: Wave, leasing, bank loan, tontine

The 5 real-world ways to finance a solar system in Senegal in 2026, with monthly payments, rates and required paperwork.

Why finance instead of paying cash?

A solar installation of USD 3,000 to 17,000 is a significant one-time outlay. Financing lets you pay for the system with the savings it generates: your old Senelec bill disappears and funds the loan instalment. In most cases the loan instalment is lower than the former bill — you save from month one. In Senegal, five routes exist.

1. Instalment plan with Dieye Solar Group

For projects of USD 3,500 to 25,000, DSG offers payment in 3 to 6 instalments over 3 to 6 months: 40 % at order, 40 % on equipment delivery, 20 % at commissioning. No interest, no credit organisation to engage. This option suits villas, shops and SMEs with the funds but preferring not to tie everything up at once.

2. Wave — first transfer to lock the order

For small kits (under USD 3,500) and ProSolar Academy enrollment fees, Wave accepts transfers up to roughly USD 1,700/day. Pay in several transfers if needed, with no fee on the customer side. DSG uses this flow to validate orders and formalise a deposit.

3. Green bank loan

Several Senegalese banks offer solar equipment loans at preferential rates: BOA, Ecobank, SGBS, CBAO. Terms run 24 to 60 months, with effective rates of 7-12 %/year. For a USD 8,500 installation over 36 months at 9 %, monthly payment is around USD 270 — compare that to the USD 250-500 in Senelec bills it erases. Banque Agricole (ex-CNCAS) offers loans dedicated to agricultural projects (solar pumping, irrigation) with even more favourable terms.

4. Solar leasing

A few operators offer a contract where they install and keep ownership of the equipment for 3-7 years. You pay a monthly rent slightly below your Senelec bill, then take full ownership at contract end. Interesting for SMEs and houses of worship that don't want to carry the upfront investment. DSG works with several leasing partners — ask us for an introduction.

5. Solar tontine and community financing

For small rural installations and villages, dedicated tontines exist: 10-20 households contribute monthly and each in turn gets their solar kit. The solar tontine fits the Senegalese tradition of community savings. It is often paired with the training of local technicians who handle maintenance — exactly what ProSolar Academy prepares with the next cohort in December 2026.

What paperwork for a financing application?

For a bank loan: ID, last 3 payslips or bank statements, last 3 Senelec invoices, dated and signed DSG quote. The bank checks your instalment stays below 33 % of your income. For leasing: a quote and an active electricity account are often enough, without heavy solvency analysis. For DSG instalments: a simple deposit and a signed order form.

Frequently asked questions

Can you pay for a solar installation in instalments in Senegal?
Yes. Dieye Solar Group offers 3 to 6 interest-free instalments. Senegalese banks also offer solar equipment loans over 24-60 months at 7-12 %/year. Wave accepts payments up to ~USD 1,700/day.
Which Senegalese bank offers solar loans?
BOA, Ecobank, SGBS, CBAO and Banque Agricole (ex-CNCAS) all offer solar loans. Banque Agricole is particularly suited to agricultural projects (pumping, irrigation). DSG can refer you to an advisor.
What does a monthly solar loan payment look like?
For a USD 8,500 installation over 36 months at 9 %, monthly payment is around USD 270. Compare to your current Senelec bill: the instalment is often equal to or less than it, and you own the system after 3 years.
